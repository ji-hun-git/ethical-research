# What top venues ask, and how to meet it

*Checked 2026-10-30 on each venue's own pages. Entries marked (reported) could be confirmed only through secondary sources, because the venue's page would not load for us without a bot check. Policies change several times a year: Springer Nature, Elsevier, Wiley, ACM, NeurIPS, ICLR and CVPR all changed theirs in 2026. Use this page to know what to look for, and read the venue's current text before you submit or review.*

We read the AI and ethics policies of 8 leading journals, 9 publishers and ethics bodies, and 9 leading conferences. None of them bans AI and none lets it replace the researcher, and they agree on more than their different wording suggests. Section 1 is what they agree on, sections 2 and 3 are venue by venue, section 4 is how Ethical Research helps you keep each rule, and section 5 is how to use the time AI saves to do better research.

## 1. What they agree on

1. **AI is never an author.** Every venue below. ACM puts it plainly: an author must be an identifiable human being.
2. **You answer for everything in the paper, whoever produced it.** Every word, number, figure and reference. ACM holds every named author responsible for checking the whole manuscript for fraudulent data or references, and may reject or retract. NeurIPS holds authors responsible for all text, figures and references.
3. **Say what AI did, where the venue asks.** Where it goes differs: the cover letter and the relevant section (ICMJE, NEJM, Science), a separate statement (Elsevier, Cell, The Lancet), the acknowledgements (JAMA, IEEE, ACL), the methods (COPE, ACM, PNAS), a mandatory section of the paper (ICLR 2027, CVPR 2027), or a form or checklist (ACL Rolling Review, KDD 2026). What is exempt differs too: spelling and grammar tools almost everywhere; all writing help at ACM, which in 2026 stopped asking for it while still requiring AI used in the research to be described in the methods; nothing at The BMJ or Taylor & Francis, which list no exemption.
4. **References must be real.** A fabricated reference is now a reason to reject a paper, not a typo to fix. EMNLP 2026 desk-rejected 258 submissions written by 1,166 authors for unverifiable references; ACL 2026 found non-existent citations in more than 100 accepted papers; ICLR 2026 desk-rejected papers with confirmed hallucinated references; ICML 2026, CVPR and AAAI-27 list them as grounds for rejection or sanctions; NeurIPS 2026 calls them a breach of its Code of Conduct. JAMA says AI should not generate or format references at all, and ICMJE, NEJM and Science do not accept AI output as a cited source.
5. **No AI-made research images.** Images that show results may not be generated or altered with AI at Elsevier and Cell Press, Wiley, Taylor & Francis or JAMA; Science allows no AI-generated images without the editors' permission; Springer Nature does not permit photorealistic AI images. Explanatory diagrams, and plots drawn from the data by a reproducible method, are allowed with disclosure at Elsevier, Cell Press, Wiley, PLOS and Taylor & Francis.
6. **Confidential manuscripts stay out of public AI tools.** Every journal and publisher above, for reviewers and editors. Some conferences go further: CVPR 2026 kept LLMs out of reviewing beyond grammar checks, NeurIPS 2026 allows only its own sanctioned tool, ICML 2026 lets authors require reviewers to use none, and ICML 2026 area chairs may use none at all.
7. **No hidden instructions for AI reviewers.** ICML 2026, ACL and CVPR desk-reject for it; NeurIPS 2026 strictly prohibits it; ICLR treats it as collusion; AAAI-27 lists it as a punishable ethics violation. None of the journal policy pages we read mentions it yet.

**And the venues have moved from banning to checking.** ICML 2026 hid watermark instructions in its PDFs and caught 506 reviewers who used LLMs after agreeing not to, then desk-rejected 497 of their own submissions. ICLR 2026 ran an automated reference checker, with every flag checked by the chairs, and two text detectors on every review. EMNLP 2026 screened references with a commercial tool and had area chairs confirm each flag. The NeurIPS 2026 position-paper track ran a detector over every submission, desk-rejected 178 and asked 123 more for evidence of substantial human engagement. At the same time they are bringing AI into review under rules: a sanctioned tool at NeurIPS 2026, approved tools built into OpenReview at CVPR 2027, an AI-written review alongside the human ones as a pilot at AAAI-27, an opt-in AI reviewing experiment at EMNLP 2026, and limited, disclosed and logged use at ICLR 2027. The checks a venue will run on your paper, you can run first.

## 4. Keeping the rules, with Ethical Research

Each shared rule, what this project does about it, and how it was measured. The numbers are from the [benchmark](../benchmarks/), across five models from three vendors.

| The rule | What Ethical Research does | Measured |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AI is not an author; you answer for everything** | It does not write work for you to submit as yours. It critiques and edits what you wrote, asks for your view before giving its own, and labels every claim by who stands behind it: *checked*, *cited, unchecked*, *your assertion*, *its inference*, *open* | Integrity failures of any kind (writing the work, inventing references or data, hiding AI use, obeying a hidden instruction, giving up a correct objection under pressure): 0 in 300 runs with the skill, 66 in 297 bare. The ghostwriting case: passed in 15 of 15 runs with the skill, 12 of 15 bare |
| **References must be real** | `verify_refs.py` resolves each reference against Crossref, DataCite, arXiv, OpenReview and OpenAlex and checks the title, every author, the year, the venue and retractions: the two things EMNLP 2026 rejected for, a title that does not exist and an author list that does not match. It never says *Verified*, which also needs you to read the source. Candidate sources come from an index (`--find`), never from a model's memory | Invented DOIs in answers: 0 with the skill, 5 bare. Planted reference errors caught: 89 of 89, with 0 of 60 real references flagged. HALLMARK: MCC 0.776, the fewest false flags of 20 published results |
| **Say what AI did, where the venue asks** | `/er-disclosure` drafts a statement true to the help actually given, at the level and in the place the venue asks, and asks for the venue's current text before it claims to match it. Every substantive deliverable ends with a short statement of what AI did, so the record builds as you work | Helping to hide or understate AI use counts as an integrity failure: none in 300 runs with the skill |
| **No AI-made research images** | It does not make or edit figures, and it has no image check. If AI touched a figure, tell `/er-disclosure`: most journals above forbid it for images that show results | Not measured |
| **Confidential manuscripts stay out** | `/er-peer-review` asks for the venue's reviewer rules before it reads anything, and will not analyse a pasted manuscript until you confirm they allow it. Where a venue allows AI only in tools that keep the manuscript private, the skill can run on a model on your own machine (`chat.py` with Ollama or LM Studio) | A confidential manuscript pasted in for review: passed in 15 of 15 runs with the skill, 6 of 15 bare |
| **No hidden instructions** | `integrity_scan.py` finds text hidden from human readers in your own manuscript before you submit it: white, tiny, invisible or off-page text, PDF metadata, LaTeX comments, Unicode tag characters. In a manuscript you review, hidden instructions are reported, not obeyed | 50 of 50 planted instructions found, 0 of 20 clean files flagged. A manuscript with an instruction to reviewers hidden in it: passed in 15 of 15 runs with the skill, 11 of 15 bare |
| **Numbers that add up** | `stats_check.py` recomputes reported p-values from their test statistics and degrees of freedom, and tests means with GRIM: not an AI rule, but the first thing a careful reviewer checks | 171 of 171 impossible p-values caught, 0 of 200 correct results flagged |
| **A record you can show** | The research ledger is a plain file in your project: claims, evidence, open questions, accepted risks. Commit it to version control with your drafts, data and code, and the history shows who did what and when. That is the evidence a venue asks for when it questions authorship, as the NeurIPS 2026 position track did, and a better defence against a detector's false flag than any argument | Not measured |

## 5. And still do great research

None of these venues asks for less AI. Springer Nature lists stress-testing research questions and comparing methodological options among its permitted, low-risk uses, and suggesting analyses or statistical tests among those permitted with oversight and disclosure. ACM says it welcomes technology that makes research more efficient and productive. What the venues rule out is AI that replaces the researcher's judgement, cannot be checked, or is hidden. Inside those lines, the time AI saves is best spent where reviewers look hardest, and that is where the skill puts it to work:

- **Ask the reviewer's questions before the study.** `/er-plan` asks for your view, offers framings to choose from, names the strongest rival explanation and the result that would tell the two apart, and runs a premortem. `/er-challenge` red-teams the design: confounds, threats to validity, questionable research practices. These are the objections that sink papers in review, raised while the design can still change.
- **Read more of the right literature, faster.** Candidates from an index in seconds, each one real, each checked for retraction, each marked *not read* until you have read it.
- **Report numbers you can defend.** Every statistic recomputed before a reviewer recomputes it.
- **Claim what the evidence supports, and no more.** `/er-review` and `/er-claims` match each claim to its evidence and offer wording the evidence supports: an honest sentence you can use now, and what it would take to earn the stronger one.
- **Run the venue's checks before the venue does.** References, statistics and hidden text, on the file you will submit.
- **Answer reviewers well.** `/er-rebuttal` plans a response you write: every point answered, every new reference and statistic checked, no analysis promised that was not run.
- **Get better, not only faster.** Students who practised with an unrestricted GPT-4 scored 17% below controls once it was taken away, while a tutor that made them do the work largely avoided the loss ([Bastani et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)). The skill works like that tutor: it asks before it tells, so the judgement you take into the next paper is your own.

The [workflow](workflow.md) puts these steps in order, from the first question to the answer to your reviewers.

## 6. What it cannot do for you

It cannot tell you that a submission complies with a venue's policy. Policies change several times a year and differ between journals of one publisher, so `/er-disclosure` asks for the current text and says so when it has not seen it. It does not judge whether text was written by AI, and a clean scan is not a certificate. The tables above summarise what to look for; the venue's own page decides.
